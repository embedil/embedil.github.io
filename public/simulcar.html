A toy car that drives like it has mass.

SimulCar is a Bluetooth-controlled toy car that runs a real physics model underneath — drive force, rolling resistance, aerodynamic drag, and braking — instead of just mapping a slider to a motor.

Every step runs on forces, not guesses

Each tick, the firmware integrates Newton's second law against the clock's actual elapsed time, not a fixed guess — so the model stays true no matter how the simulation loop jitters.

  • drive
    Traction, then power

    Force is capped by tyre grip at low speed and by engine power once the car is rolling, so it can't out-accelerate its own motor.

  • rolling
    Rolling resistance

    A steady drag from the tyres themselves, scaled to the car's weight — the reason it won't coast forever.

  • drag
    Aerodynamic drag

    Grows with the square of speed, so top-end acceleration tapers off the way it does in a real car.

  • brake
    Braking & holding

    At a standstill, the brake acts as a holding force — the car only pulls away once drive force beats brake plus rolling resistance.

It solves for its own top speed

Rather than hard-coding a number, the firmware finds the speed where drag and rolling resistance alone would consume all available power, using a bisection search run on every preset.
